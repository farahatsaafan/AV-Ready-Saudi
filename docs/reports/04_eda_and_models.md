## 1. Question and data
How ready is each Saudi region, by season, for autonomous vehicles? The answer is an evidence-based readiness score per region x season with its drivers. Everything is computed from the one Gold table `gold.av_readiness_region_month` (13 regions x 60 months, 100 % real data). The EDA queries are in `sql/04_analysis.sql` (ROLLUP, CUBE, GROUPING SETS, RANK, NTILE, LAG).

## 2. AV evidence: crude vs adjusted relative risks (the confounding demonstration)
Relative risk of an AV event under each weather condition vs normal weather. Crude = pooled ratio (the number used before v2). Adjusted = Mantel-Haenszel over strata (NHTSA: state x incident month; DMV: month x weekday class), 95 % CI widened by the overdispersion factor. Evidence rule: applied in Gold only if the ratio comes from AV crashes, has >= 30 events and its CI lower bound is > 1.

## 3. Readiness score (F1)
Readiness = 100 x (1/3 E + 1/3 S + 1/3 I) per region x season, over complete weather months 2021-2025.
- **E (environment)**: mean `env_readiness` = share of hours inside the US AV evidence envelope (dust weight 0, rain weight 1/RR_rain, other covered conditions 1), min-max over the 52 region-seasons.
- **S (traffic-safety environment)**: 1 - mean percent-rank of the 2021-2025 serious-accident and death rates per 100,000 (same for the 4 seasons of a region).
- **I (infrastructure)**: mean of min-max divided intercity share, road km per 1,000 people and (inverted) ambulance response time outside cities, 2022-2025.

Query E8 (Dr. Omar's comparison): 10 of 13 regions have a higher relative rate in their most obscured year (Wilcoxon signed-rank p = 0.048). The most obscured year is 2022: 12, 2023: 1 (year: regions). With the year's mean relative rate removed, 9 of 13 are higher (p = 0.273).

**Result: null.** No weather model generalises to a region it has not seen (every leave-one-region-out model is worse than the mean), the leave-one-year-out "skill" is region identity (compare the region-mean baseline, which uses no weather at all), and no year-adjusted rank correlation is significant. The apparent dust signal in the region-only correlation and in E8 is a year effect: 2022 is the most obscured year for 12 of 13 regions, and the average relative rate of the regions is itself higher in some years (2021 1.14, 2022 1.02, 2023 0.93, 2024 0.96; small regions do not move with the national rate). With annual regional accident counts, the share of dusty, hazy or rainy hours does not explain which regions or years have more serious accidents. This is a finding, not a failure: it is why readiness is built from the AV evidence envelope and the region's own safety and infrastructure record, not from a weather -> accident model.

## 7. Limitations
- Dust has no US AV evidence at all (0 NHTSA events, 0 DMV dust days): it is treated as uncovered (weight 0), not as measured risk.
- Saudi dry haze is mostly mineral dust; California's is mostly smoke: the conservative variant gives it weight 0.
- DMV disengagements have a date but no location; California weather is taken as the worse of LAX and SFO.
- NHTSA crashes have month-level dates; exposure is station-hours of the state, not AV miles. 17 of the 70 rain crashes fall in state-months where the reference station logged no rain hour (station is not the crash place).
- Accidents are annual per region; monthly rows repeat them (use one row per region-year).
- Heat: hours >= 40 degC are 6.1 % in Saudi and 7.5 % at Phoenix, where AV fleets run commercially, but there is no event-level heat evidence.
- The 2025 workbook revises the 2024 rates; each year uses its own publication.
- NOAA stopped updating its hourly product (ISD) after 24 Aug 2025: September-December 2025 has no weather.
- One weather station per region (the main airport).
- Association is not causation: the score is an evidence-based estimate of readiness, not a measurement.
